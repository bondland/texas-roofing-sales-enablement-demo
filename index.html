Homeowner checklist

Your immediate next steps. A calm, practical flow helps homeowners act safely without pretending that a contractor controls the insurance decision.

If it is safe, take photos of visible exterior damage. Do not climb onto the roof.

For an immediate safety risk, call 911 or the appropriate emergency service. For an active leak, take safe, reasonable steps to limit further damage and contact your carrier for guidance.

Have your policy information and recent storm details available for your conversations.

Document observable conditions and discuss the repair work your contractor recommends.

Ask about reporting requirements, policy terms, and the claims process.

Clear boundaries

Two roles
One clearer customer experience

Clarity prevents an estimator from improvising and gives the homeowner a reliable explanation to share with family or an insurance carrier.

What the contractor can help with

  • Inspecting roof conditions
  • Documenting observable damage
  • Providing photos, measurements, and a proposed repair scope
  • Explaining the work recommended
  • Scheduling repairs after a signed agreement

What the insurance carrier handles

  • Coverage decisions under the policy
  • Claim review and payment decisions
  • Policy terms, limits, and deductibles
  • Its estimate and any approved claim scope

A contractor can explain its proposed repair scope. The insurance carrier decides coverage under the policy.

Texas compliance

Trust grows when the page defines the line

Compliance should not feel like a legal warning at the bottom of a page. It should be part of the customer experience: plain language, right expectations, and a clear next step.

Your deductible

Your deductible is part of your insurance policy and remains your responsibility. Texas law does not allow contractors to offer to waive, rebate, or absorb a deductible.

Questions about deductible, coverage, or a claim decision should go directly to the insurance carrier.
